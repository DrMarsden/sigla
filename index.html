A collator for the witnesses of a text: variants, transpositions and an apparatus, in verse or prose.

Vibe-coded by Steve Marsden and Claude

Paste two or more versions of a poem or a passage of prose. Sigla shows where they differ, line by line and word by word, and sets out the differences as a scholarly apparatus.

Help

A first collation

  1. Paste each version of the text into its own box and give each a siglum (a short label such as A or 1856). To paste several at once, use “Paste several witnesses at once”.
  2. Choose the base text: the version every other one is measured against.
  3. Tap Collate. The Text tab shows the base text with every difference marked; the Apparatus tab lists them.

Tap “Load an example” to see a finished collation before pasting anything.

Words used here

Witness
One version of the text: an edition, a magazine printing, a manuscript.
Siglum (plural sigla)
The short label that stands for a witness in the apparatus.
Base text
The witness the others are compared with. Choosing it is a convenience and makes no claim that it is the best or the earliest.
Variant
A place where a witness reads differently from the base text.
Lemma
The base-text reading, printed before the bracket in an entry: lemma] A; other reading B.
Substantive
A variant that changes the wording.
Accidental
A variant in punctuation, capitals, word division or spelling only.
Apparatus
The full list of variants.
Turnover
The run-on part of a verse line too long for the page, printed indented on the next line.
Transposition
The same line or passage standing in a different place.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Each witness is compared with the base text separately. Two witnesses are grouped only when their readings are the same.
  • It works best on verse. In prose, a sentence moved inside a paragraph shows as a cut and an insertion.
  • Line matching is a judgement. Check the matches the Line matching tab marks as doubtful, and correct them there.
  • A reading is only as good as the transcription pasted in. Check surprising readings against the source.
  • Substantive and accidental are assigned by rule; change any you disagree with in the Apparatus tab.

Take the tool with you

Sigla is one web page. Copy it out to keep your own copy, host it elsewhere, or have its method reviewed.

Paste several witnesses at once

Paste the whole block. Each witness must start with a header line; the header becomes its description.

Settings

Proofreading comparison is for two texts that ought to match, such as two transcriptions of one edition: it stops treating quote and dash forms as equal.

Spelling variants to count as accidental

One group per line, joined with = signs. A reading that differs from the base only in these spellings is classed as accidental.

Agreement between witnesses

For each pair: the places of substantive variation where the two read alike, out of the places where both are present.

Tap a marked passage for its readings, or a line number to see that line in every witness. “Key” explains the marks.

Each column is one witness, in the order of the witness boxes. Tap a line to follow it across them.

Tap “substantive” or “accidental” on an entry to change its class. Tap “note” to attach a comment that travels into the exports.

Tap a base line, then the witness line it should match; × breaks a match. Outlined matches say why they are doubtful.

Witness dates are set beside each witness, under “Edit witnesses and settings”. Notes are added in the Apparatus tab.

Edition

A web page laid out like a printed edition: witnesses, a note on the text, the base text with line numbers, then the historical collation, whole-line differences and accidentals. Open it and print to PDF.

TEI XML

The apparatus in TEI parallel segmentation, for a press, a repository or another tool. When Sigla runs inside Claude it saves with a .txt ending, because .xml files cannot be saved there; rename it afterwards.

Briefing for an AI model

One Markdown file holding everything a model needs to reason about the collation: your questions, the witnesses, how the texts were prepared, a line-by-line correspondence table, the complete apparatus and the witness texts.
